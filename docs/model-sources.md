**Proposed values are recommendations for team review, not implemented settings.** **Defend** means the exact number has not been established for a suited astronaut on Mars by the sources examined. A related paper supports context, not necessarily the coefficient. **Proxy** identifies transfer from Earth, the Moon or another Mars site. **Design** identifies software or scenario choices that do not need to masquerade as measured constants.

## Decisions for the lead

1. **Keep the 20-degree cutoff labelled as a lunar proxy.** Full-PDF checking supports the cited capability, not a Mars flight rule. [Coan 2021](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210000827)
2. **Separate inventory, metabolic allocation and backup.** The engine budgets metabolism, omitting separate leakage/purge losses and backup operation. Its 0.40 kg allocation is not validated total suit endurance. [Jeng et al. 2009](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20090013081)
3. **Treat Apollo workload as a comparison, not a fitted Mars walking law.** The report's 234 kcal/h is a whole-EVA average; geology/deployment is 244 kcal/h. Using 4184 J per kcal and 3600 seconds per hour gives 272 and 284 W respectively. Those arithmetic conversions do not establish Mars gravity or suit adjustments. [NASA metabolism report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930074290/downloads/19930074290.pdf)
4. **Review coupled speed and power before importing coefficients.** On fully penalized sand, current code implies a metabolic energy-per-distance factor of `1.4 / 0.6 = 2.33` relative to its firm-ground scenario, before other interactions. This is our algebraic inference, not validation by an Earth matched-speed experiment. The sand paper does not justify both factors separately. [Lejeune et al. 1998](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.201.13.2071)
5. **Preserve radiation metric distinctions.** RAD's surface dose equivalent is not identical to NASA's organ dose or NASA effective-dose compliance metric. Neither the measured historical baseline nor the educational 1 mSv allocation proves a walk is medically safe. The SEP factor should be labelled a stress scenario. [Hassler et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1244797), [NASA radiation requirements](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/4-0-human-performance/)
6. **Keep the Go/No-Go thresholds visibly assumed.** Unknown data already causes CAUTION. NASA's DONKI catalogue is not a Mars dosimeter or a forecast of how long an event remains hazardous. Human-selected recency windows must not imply that quiet means a Mars all-clear. [NASA CCMC DONKI](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/tools/DONKI/)

## Follow-up experiments, not sourced replacements

The lead can compare the existing scenario with a slower 0.56 m/s walking scenario and an Apollo geology-task comparison near 284 W, recording whether Jezero/Gale routes still fit. That would be a sensitivity analysis, not validation. A fitted Mars model would need a specified astronaut/suit/load, gravity and terrain conditions, speed-dependent power and held-out observations. Do not substitute an Earth cost-of-transport polynomial directly into a power coefficient without reconciling units and avoiding duplicated grade penalties.
